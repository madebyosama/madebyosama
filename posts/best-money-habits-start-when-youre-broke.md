People tend to think good money habits come after the raise, the new job or the tax refund. It usually works the other way. The habits you build with very little money are the ones that decide what happens when you have more. If you're starting over, here are six that cost nothing and work from the very next payday.

## 1. Hide money from yourself

Open a savings account at a different bank from your checking account. Then set up an automatic transfer every payday, even if it's only $5.

The amount matters less than the distance. When your savings sit next to your checking balance every time you log in, they start to look like spending money. A separate bank takes them out of sight, and an automatic transfer means you never have to decide to save. Over time, money you rarely look at is money you rarely spend.

**Try this:** Choose a bank with no minimum balance and no monthly fee. Schedule the transfer for the day your paycheck lands, before anything else can take it.

## 2. Stop saying "it's only $5"

The big purchases rarely sink a tight budget. You notice those and plan for them. The damage comes from small ones that don't feel like decisions: a snack at the register, a delivery fee, an app upgrade.

Five $5 purchases a week comes to more than $100 a month, and most of it disappears without you remembering where it went. "It's only $5" is true once. Repeated, it becomes one of the biggest lines in your budget.

**Try this:** For one week, write down every purchase under $10. Don't judge it, just record it. Most people are surprised by the total.

## 3. Ask, "Is that your best price?"

Phone, internet and insurance bills feel fixed, but they often aren't. Companies keep promotions, loyalty discounts and lower-tier plans that they won't offer unless you ask.

Call and ask plainly: "Is there a lower rate or a promotion I qualify for?" The worst answer is no. Often the answer is yes, because keeping you as a customer costs them less than finding a new one. One fifteen-minute call can cut a bill for a whole year.

**Try this:** Choose one recurring bill this week. Before you call, check what competitors charge so you have a number to point to.

## 4. Set the reminder the day you start the free trial

Free trials are built on the expectation that you'll forget them. Signing up is easy, and the charge arrives quietly weeks later.

Set a phone reminder the moment you start a trial, timed a few days before it ends. When the reminder goes off, ask one question: would I pay full price for this? If not, cancel on the spot.

**Try this:** Look through your bank statement for subscriptions you forgot about. Cancel anything you wouldn't sign up for again today.

## 5. Use cash for the thing you overspend on

Everyone has one category that gets away from them, whether it's takeout, the beauty supply store or the hair store. For that category, switch to cash.

Each payday, take out a set amount for it. When the cash runs out, you're done until the next payday. It works because handing over real bills feels different from tapping a card. You see the money leave, and you see how much is left.

**Try this:** Keep your cash for this category in its own envelope, separate from everything else in your wallet.

## 6. Treat $20 like it's $2,000

This habit holds the other five together. How you handle a small amount is practice for how you'll handle a large one. If every dollar disappears now, a bigger paycheck will disappear the same way, only faster.

So give every dollar a plan, however small the check. A plan for $20 might be $10 to savings, $5 to a bill and $5 to spend freely. That still counts as a budget, and it builds the skill you'll need when the numbers grow.

## Start with one

You don't need to do all six this week. Pick the one that hits closest to home and start there. Small, steady habits built while money is tight are what carry you once it isn't.
